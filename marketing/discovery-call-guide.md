# Customer discovery call guide

Written 2026-08-14 for the call with Michael Milton's co-founder (a lawyer who
handles all their contracting). Reusable for the paying customer and the 29
non-activators.

## The one rule

**Do not pitch.** The entire value of this call is hearing how he works and
what he objects to. The moment you describe SignedBy, he starts being polite,
and polite people tell you what you want to hear. Everything after that is
worthless.

If he asks what SignedBy does, answer in one sentence and get back to
questions. You can demo another day — you can only get a clean first
description of his world once.

## Opening (30 seconds)

> Thanks for the time. I'm not going to pitch you anything — I'm trying to
> understand what a lawyer actually needs from a signing tool, because I'd
> rather hear where ours falls short than guess. Mind if I just ask about how
> you work?

Then mention Amsterdam briefly if it's natural. Warmth, not five minutes.

## Questions that work

Facts and stories, not opinions. In rough order:

1. **What do you use today to get things signed?**
2. **Walk me through the last contract you sent out — what actually happened,
   step by step?** (Best question on the list. Let him talk. Don't interrupt.)
3. **What's the worst part of that process?**
4. **Have you ever switched tools? What triggered it?** (Switching triggers are
   how you find the moment a competitor becomes replaceable.)
5. **Who decides which tool gets used — you, or the other side?** (Critical in
   legal. If counterparties dictate, that changes SignedBy's whole go-to-market.)
6. **Do you know what you pay for it?** (If he doesn't know, price isn't the
   pain — which would undercut the entire /vs/docusign positioning.)
7. **Has a counterparty ever refused or questioned a tool you used?**
8. **What would have to be true for you to move off what you use now?**

### Lawyer-specific, worth the time

9. **What standard do you actually need — is a simple e-signature fine, or do
   some documents need more?** (Probes SES vs AdES/QES under eIDAS. SignedBy is
   SES + RFC 3161 timestamp; if he needs AdES routinely, that's a real gap and
   better to know now.)
10. **Would you put a client matter through a tool you'd never heard of?**
    (Brutal but the most important question on this page. Brand trust is a
    genuine barrier in legal work, and if the answer is no, that shapes
    everything.)
11. **What do you need from the audit trail if something is ever disputed?**

## Questions that don't work — avoid

- "Would you use X?" / "Would you pay for Y?" — hypothetical, and people are
  kind. Worthless data.
- "Do you think this is a good idea?" — invites flattery.
- Anything that describes SignedBy before he's described his world.
- Leading questions: "Isn't DocuSign expensive?" Let him raise price, or not.

## What to listen for

- **Does he mention price unprompted?** If not, price isn't his pain — and
  that's a significant finding given the homepage leads with it.
- **Does he name DocuSign?** What tone?
- **Does he describe a workaround?** Workarounds are the strongest evidence of
  real pain — people only build them around things that genuinely hurt.
- **Emotion.** Irritation, resignation, a sigh. Flat description means low
  pain. Note the feeling, not just the fact.

## Close

- **Ask for 15 minutes, take 15.** Ending early builds enormous credibility and
  makes the second conversation easy.
- Finish with: **"Who else should I be talking to?"** — the cheapest source of
  the next three calls.
- **Don't send a pitch afterwards.** Send a thank-you and one specific thing
  you took from it. Sell later, if at all.

## Afterwards

Write it up within the hour, while the phrasing is fresh. Capture his actual
words rather than your summary of them — "we just email PDFs back and forth,
honestly" is worth more than "uses email."

Look for the same phrase appearing across several calls. One person's opinion
is an anecdote; the same sentence from three people is a finding.
